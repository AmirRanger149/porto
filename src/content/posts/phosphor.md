## Scanlines are a trust protocol

Open any SaaS landing page: soft gradients, rounded everything, a sans-serif
whispering that nothing here can hurt you. Now open a terminal. It says the
opposite — *everything here can hurt you, and I will show you exactly what
happens when you type*. That candor is the aesthetic. The phosphor glow, the
hard edges, the block cursor waiting like a held breath: it's interface as
**contract**, not interface as sedative.

## The CRT cheats your eye honestly

Why does green-on-black feel "alive" while flat dark mode feels dead?

- **Scanlines** give the eye texture to resolve — motion reads as signal,
  not noise
- **One saturated color + two accents** forces hierarchy; you can't hide a
  weak layout behind a palette
- **Monospace type** makes structure visible — indentation *is* information

The trick is restraint. This site's rain runs at 30% opacity behind the
content, not on top of it. The CRT sweep crosses once every ten seconds.
Nostalgia at full volume is a screensaver; at low volume it's atmosphere.

## Borrow the discipline, skip the cosplay

You don't need katakana glyphs to take the lesson: **let the interface admit
what it is**. A button that inverts on hover says "I am a switch." A log
that scrolls says "time passed." Ugly green isn't a color scheme — it's the
sound of a machine agreeing to be watched.
